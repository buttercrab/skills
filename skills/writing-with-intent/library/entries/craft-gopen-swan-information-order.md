## When to use

Sentences are grammatical but the reader cannot tell what connects them, which finding matters, or whose action is being described. Ask the author which relationship and emphasis the passage intends before rearranging it.

## What to inspect

Inspect how the article's revisions expose substantive missing links rather than merely shortening prose. Its topic-position discussion accepts passive constructions when they maintain the paragraph's subject. Its conclusion explicitly rejects fixed algorithms and allows purposeful violations.

## What to borrow and adapt

Mark each sentence's starting context, main action and intended emphasis. In an explanation of a queue backlog, keep the backlog as the thread when explaining its consequences; switch to workers when their behavior becomes the subject. If a transition requires an unsupported causal claim, flag the missing explanation and request evidence instead of manufacturing a bridge.

## Mismatches and limits

This is a craft argument illustrated with scientific passages, not a controlled reader experiment. Interpretive revisions can change intended meaning. Familiar-to-new order is a diagnostic, not a requirement that all new information appear last. Neither shortness nor active voice guarantees clarity.

# Toss es-toolkit: connect engineering decisions to adoption friction

## When to use
An engineering retrospective needs both a technical decision and the author's actual experience.

## What to inspect
Compare the named-speaker headings, specific utility functions, contributor passage, and compatibility discussion. The English page explicitly identifies itself as a version of the Korean article.

## Craft trace
Reader question: why did adoption require more than faster code? The opening establishes practitioner roles; the compatibility passage identifies scattered call sites and behavior differences before introducing the intermediate layer. That sequence makes a design choice answer a concrete migration obstacle. The named contributor passage supplies a second perspective rather than an anonymous corporate narrator. Transfer the decision-to-obstacle relationship, not the author's life story or signature cadence.

## Revision implications
Our proposed transfer: replace an unsupported success adjective with the actual constraint and decision it conceals. Request the writer's evidence and lived details; never manufacture them. Keep technical caveats during translation and agree the target speech level. No author draft history was inspected.

## What to borrow and adapt
Outline one reader problem, the failed assumption, the chosen intervention, and remaining proof needed. Compare bilingual equivalents for meaning and attribution rather than word-for-word sameness.

## Mismatches and limits
This is an interested participant's company publication, not independent evaluation. Do not repeat its performance or popularity claims as verified findings. These passages do not establish universal Korean or English preferences. Images and the complete ending were not analyzed.

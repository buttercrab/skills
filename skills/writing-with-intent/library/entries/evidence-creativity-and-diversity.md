## When to use

Use when deciding whether an idea assistant should supply an angle before the writer has made one. Ask: Is the aim a stronger single draft, a distinctive viewpoint, or varied pieces across a publication?

## What to inspect

The analysis contains 293 writers total across three arms, not 293 AI-treated writers. UK Prolific participants wrote eight-sentence English stories; treatment offered one or up to five GPT-4 ideas. Separate evaluators rated the stories. Access to ideas, particularly five, increased rated novelty/usefulness; embedding comparisons also found greater similarity across AI-assisted stories.

## What to borrow and adapt

Our proposed workflow: collect the author's experience, claim and intended surprise before showing suggestions. Compare two or three genuinely different angles against those inputs. If publishing a series, compare its premises and examples across pieces, since one attractive draft cannot establish variety across the collection.

## Mismatches and limits

These were constrained short stories by participants not selected as professional writers. Ratings and embedding similarity answer different questions; neither establishes literary merit, plagiarism or authorship. Long-run publishing effects are extrapolations, not measured outcomes. Asking for author inputs is our application, not an intervention tested here.

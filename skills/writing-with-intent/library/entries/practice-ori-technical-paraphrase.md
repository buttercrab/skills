# ORI: a paraphrase can change the technical claim

## When to use
A writer summarizes a technical process they only partly understand, especially across languages.

## What to inspect
The guide juxtaposes a cited biomedical passage, an attempted paraphrase, and a prior guide version it identifies as subtly mistaken. It explains why specialist terms resist ordinary synonym replacement.

## Craft trace
Reader question: why is changing wording insufficient? The discussion moves from a general difficulty to a worked case, then exposes an error in the guide's own earlier example. Making that limitation visible prevents the illustration from masquerading as infallible authority. Transfer the practice of checking conceptual roles before polishing expression.

## Revision implications
The embedded versions are inspected revision evidence as presented by the guide; they are not independently authenticated archival drafts. Our transfer: map entities, actions, direction and conditions, then ask a domain reader to check meaning. Preserve necessary terminology and explain it; do not chase lexical novelty. No biomedical interpretation is asserted here.

## What to borrow and adapt
Annotate a paraphrase with what it preserves, omits and infers. Request clarification when the writer cannot explain a relationship. A citation does not repair a misrepresentation.

## Mismatches and limits
The cited experiments and original Science paper were not read, so this card makes no empirical-effect claim. The guide's discussion is educational, not permission to copy substantial passages or a general legal exemption.

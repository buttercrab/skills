# Reuters: sourcing and calibrated claims

## When to use
A draft reports external events, disputed claims, interviews, or another writer's findings.

## What to inspect
The sourcing section joins verification to source identity and motive; the quotes section treats translation and context as accuracy questions. Corrections are public accountability rather than cosmetic editing.

## What to borrow and adapt
For every consequential assertion, ask: Who directly knows this? What supports it? What could disprove it? Mark the draft's statements as observed fact, attributed claim, our interpretation, or hypothetical illustration. This marking system is our editorial adaptation, not a Reuters taxonomy. Keep attribution adjacent to the claim; disclose material gaps instead of smoothing them away. For translated interviews, retain the original recording/text and record who translated it.

## Mismatches and limits
These are Reuters newsroom rules, including special approval procedures for exceptional anonymous sourcing. They do not establish every publication's policies. An opinion essay may argue openly; it still needs honest provenance for factual premises. Source counts alone do not establish independence or truth. Never invent an anonymous source or turn uncertain evidence into certainty to improve the rhythm.

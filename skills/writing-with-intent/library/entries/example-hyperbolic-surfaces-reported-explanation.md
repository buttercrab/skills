## When to use

When a research story risks becoming either an inaccessible proof summary or a personality profile detached from the work.

## What to inspect

The early review-status qualification, recurring technical definitions, stalled approach, borrowed method and return to the legacy frame.

## Craft trace

Question: what did later researchers make possible, and why was it difficult? Human chronology gives orientation; definitions arrive when the next obstacle requires them. Attributed voices distinguish recollection and assessment from the reporter’s explanatory transitions.

## Revision implications

Our proposed transfer: build an obstacle map before an outline. For every biographical scene, name the technical question it helps readers follow; for every technical paragraph, identify the next development it unlocks. Put review status near the result.

## What to borrow and adapt

Borrow progressive explanation and attribution. Give claims an evidence path, then let the ending return to stakes without inflating certainty.

## Mismatches and limits

This card evaluates reporting craft, not the theorem or current peer-review status. Reported recollections cannot establish private motives. Correction notices document amendments but do not expose a full original draft or revision process.

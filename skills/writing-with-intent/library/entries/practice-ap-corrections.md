# AP: visible corrections and factual boundaries

## When to use
A published article needs a substantive factual correction, or a draft uses imagined people, scenarios, reconstructed scenes, or stock imagery.

## What to inspect
AP requires identifiable corrections and rejects fabricated reporting. Its visual guidance also considers whether a generic image falsely implies a connection to the specific story.

## What to borrow and adapt
Before publication, identify the correction owner and update location. A useful correction names the erroneous assertion, the accurate replacement, and the affected conclusion. Preserve enough context for a returning reader to understand the change. During drafting, label invented examples as hypothetical where they first appear; distinguish witnessed action, sourced reconstruction, interpretation, and illustration. This drafting classification is our transfer, not AP's official classification.

## Mismatches and limits
AP's prohibition on composite characters belongs to factual news reporting; fiction and clearly disclosed teaching scenarios have different contracts. Do not import all newsroom restrictions into a personal essay. This resource is an editorial policy, not legal advice or a controlled study. A typographical repair and a factual correction have different effects on the reader; do not hide the latter as generic cleanup.

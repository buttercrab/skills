## When to use

A rich narrative has many scenes but no clear basis for choosing among them, or the writer is stuck polishing the opening. Ask what the material currently seems to mean and which tensions the writer wants the reader to experience.

## What to inspect

Inspect the difference between a backstage theme and a published nut graf. Hart describes a provisional statement guiding emphasis and selection, permits drafting elsewhere before the opening, and allows the theme to change when reporting changes.

## What to borrow and adapt

For a feature about a community repair workshop, compare two possible focuses: practical skills becoming shared resources, or disagreement over who can access them. Each would select different scenes and require different evidence. Let the user choose the meaningful direction, then test it against the material. Keep a private focus sentence; revise or discard it when the evidence resists.

## Mismatches and limits

This is a narrative editor's method and judgment, not proof that all writing needs one explicit theme or a three-word causal slogan. An exploratory essay may need several unresolved centers. A provisional theme must not justify excluding contradictory evidence or inventing causality. The statement need not appear in the article, and drafting order is a writer's choice.

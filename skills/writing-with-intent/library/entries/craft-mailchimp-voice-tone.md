## When to use

A draft swings between solemn and jokey, or a requested friendly tone becomes forced humor. Ask how the writer normally sounds, how the reader is likely to feel, and what the situation demands.

## What to inspect

Read the distinction between an enduring voice and a tone that responds to circumstances. Inspect the guide's restraint around humor alongside its distinctly Mailchimp character and sentence-level preferences.

## What to borrow and adapt

Build a brief from the user's own prose: directness, rhythm, distance from the reader and permissible humor. An article explaining a missed deadline might retain the author's conversational cadence while using precise, restrained language around consequences. A light aside may fit a low-stakes tutorial; the same aside can distract from a reader's urgent problem. Compare both choices with the user when the emotional intent is unclear.

## Mismatches and limits

This is one company's identity system, not a neutral definition of effective writing. Do not transplant its character, humor or active-voice preference wholesale. Tone judgments depend on genre, culture, relationship and reader circumstance. Stable voice does not mean an author must sound identical in a personal essay and a policy analysis, and craft inspection does not establish how actual readers feel.

# ORI: cite the work actually consulted

## When to use
An article synthesizes studies, depends on secondhand claims, or paraphrases someone else's explanation.

## What to inspect
The list distinguishes attribution, faithful paraphrase, citation accuracy, actual consultation, and fair treatment of contrary evidence. A bibliography entry alone does not tell readers which nearby idea belongs to whom.

## What to borrow and adapt
Keep three note fields: inspected original, inspected secondary account, and uninspected lead. When only the secondary account is available, describe that limitation rather than pretending to have read the original. Draft from an understood claim outline, then compare meaning and attribution against the source. Check that every citation supports its particular assertion. Preserve qualifications and relevant disagreement. Ask the writer what conclusion is theirs; label that inference separately.

## Mismatches and limits
This is educational scholarly guidance hosted by a research-integrity institution, not a universal legal definition or a plagiarism detector. Its list includes disciplinary publication practices that may not apply to a general article. A low similarity score cannot establish integrity; shared technical terminology can also be necessary. Do not use the list to accuse an author without examining actual text, context and applicable standards.

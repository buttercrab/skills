## When to use

A paragraph drops a quotation or statistic and moves on, or contains several unrelated moves. Ask what the paragraph contributes and what the reader must infer from its evidence.

## What to inspect

Read the handout's account of unity and coherence alongside its multiple organization possibilities. It explicitly separates a paragraph from fixed length and allows topic sentences in different positions. The worked development sequence is an illustration, not a required mold.

## What to borrow and adapt

After a finding, supply the interpretation the argument actually needs. For example, a count of closed shops does not by itself show why a street became less accessible: connect the measure to the relevant service, distance or population, with support. Conversely, a paragraph that establishes atmosphere may need precise observed detail rather than an explicit argumentative explanation after every sentence.

## Mismatches and limits

The source primarily serves student academic writing. Applying its example mechanically can produce repetitive claim-example-explanation prose and erase suspense or rhythm. One sentence can form a purposeful paragraph; a long paragraph can remain coherent. This resource shares UNC's teaching context with the argument card and is not an independent empirical confirmation of it.

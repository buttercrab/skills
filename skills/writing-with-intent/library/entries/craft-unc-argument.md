## When to use

An opinion piece has abundant facts but an unclear conclusion, or its examples do not establish the conclusion being drawn. Ask what a reasonable reader could dispute and what would change the writer's mind.

## What to inspect

The handout separates a position from mere topic coverage, treats evidence standards as field-dependent, and recommends engaging meaningful opposing views. Read evidence and counterargument together: both test the strength and scope of the claim.

## What to borrow and adapt

For an essay favoring a four-day workweek, distinguish employee preference from demonstrated productivity. A survey may support the first without proving the second. State a narrower conclusion, identify the missing causal evidence, or change the article's question. Present a substantial objection in terms its proponent could recognize; if it survives, revise the claim or explain the remaining uncertainty.

## Mismatches and limits

The source addresses academic argument. A personal essay need not turn every scene into proof of a contestable proposition, and a reported feature may leave multiple interpretations unresolved. Counterarguments are not a demand for equal weight to unsupported claims. Relevance analysis does not independently verify whether a source or number is true; reporting verification belongs to a separate check.

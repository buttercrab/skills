## When to use

A public argument sounds forceful but leaves the action, stakes or referent unclear. Ask what happened, who did it, and what the abstract phrase would commit the writer to if stated concretely.

## What to inspect

Inspect Orwell's argument about habitual phrases and political concealment, then read the qualifications near the end rather than isolating his numbered prescriptions. He rejects fake simplicity and makes clear that this essay's target is not literary language.

## What to borrow and adapt

In an organization-change article, replace an unsupported abstraction about improved alignment with the actual decision, responsible group and consequence when those are known. If they are unknown, keep the uncertainty visible. Test whether a metaphor clarifies a relationship or merely supplies an impressive mood; keep intentional imagery that earns its place.

## Mismatches and limits

This is a historical polemic and craft argument, not a representative study of language or evidence about AI authorship. Do not turn its preferences about vocabulary, passive voice or borrowed words into automatic bans. Some historical judgments are culturally narrow, and exact technical terms can be essential. Clear prose can still express false or harmful claims; surface clarity does not establish truth.

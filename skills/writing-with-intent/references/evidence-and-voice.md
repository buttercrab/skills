# Evidence, meaning and author voice

Use this when checking claims, integrating research, revising sourced passages or preserving a supplied author's language.

## Keep claim types visible

Observation, sourced fact, personal experience/belief, interpretation, recommendation and illustrative fiction can coexist. Their evidential promises differ. A first-person memory needs the author's account, not an invented cinematic detail. A personal judgment can be honestly argued without pretending it is a measured universal result. A sourced number needs its unit, denominator, date, population and comparison.

Check that the evidence supports the actual conclusion, not merely adjacent words. Several correct claims can still produce an unsupported inference or refer to different entities/conditions. Source disagreement may reflect versions or different questions; preserve a real unresolved conflict instead of choosing the smoother narrative.

For consequential mutable facts, inspect owning current material when permitted and needed. A reference article's technical craft does not certify its technical claims for today's task. A snippet, remembered citation or article title is not a receipt for the source body. If sources cannot be accessed under the task's limits, state the gap and narrow/qualify the claim; do not manufacture support.

Attribution should be near the claim it supports. Separate an original source from someone describing it, and the source's reported result from your interpretation. Do not cite a paper as evidence for a workflow it did not study. Keep quotations brief and exact, preserve necessary context, and link rather than reproduce protected articles. Original paraphrase still needs attribution for sourced ideas.

## Preserve meaning during editing

Inspect changes to agents, scope, conditionals, modality, negation and causal language. “Can,” “may,” “usually” and “did in this trial” are not interchangeable. Do not silently turn untested features into available capabilities, an observation into a promise, or an unresolved issue into a successful outcome.

Keep a supplied author's stance, idiom, purposeful fragments, humor and meaningful repetition when they serve the piece. Precision does not require everyone to sound alike. Technical terms can be necessary for experts; unfamiliar readers may need a bridge rather than a misleading synonym.

For line/copy editing, distinguish required fixes from optional preference and preserve the requested layer. For developmental editing, show consequential restructuring and why it serves the author's goal. If the requested edit would make a statement false or misleading, flag that conflict rather than silently obeying or replacing the whole article.

Multilingual editing requires the actual intended language/reader context. Formal public guidance, conversational essays and technical explanations can use different registers. Preserve source meaning and authorial uncertainty across languages; apparent fluency is not translation accuracy. Do not infer that literal English word order or a house English style guide governs Korean prose.

## Review tools proportionally

Grammar/style/readability tools can reveal candidates for human inspection. Their scores/flags do not establish truth, coherence, voice quality, expert accessibility or authorship. A rule set can encode one organization's preferences. Inspect tool configuration, supported language and execution effects before selecting it; no writing task implicitly authorizes uploading private prose to a hosted service.

Do not promise comprehensive fact-checking from isolated claim checks. Re-read the argument, entity relationships and qualifications after checking parts. The final piece should remain accurate as a whole, not just pass a list of local checks.

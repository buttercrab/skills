# Structure and substantive revision

Use this for article-level organization or when polished sentences fail to add up to a useful piece. These are lenses, not a required outline.

## Identify the piece's progression

For an explanation, locate the reader's question, prerequisites, faithful example/model, missing reasoning and limits. For an argument, locate the claim, reasons, evidence, serious alternative and consequence. For a narrative/personal essay, locate what changes through specific experience and reflection. For reporting, distinguish observed/reported material from interpretation and what remains unestablished.

A strong opening creates the right expectation for this piece. It need not be a hook, personal anecdote or thesis announcement. An ending should earn its place by resolving, complicating or carrying forward the central question; a summary is useful only when the reader needs it. Do not add a concluding recap automatically.

## Diagnose before polishing

Try a short reverse outline: what does each paragraph do, and what changes for the reader? Repetition can orient or develop an idea, but repeated promises and generic praise often do neither. A long sentence may need a clearer relationship, not deletion; a short sentence can still make an unsupported leap.

Find passages where a reader must supply missing logic. Put the relevant object/example before the abstraction when that helps, explain why evidence supports the conclusion, and bound analogies where their mechanisms stop matching. Keep necessary complexity; simplify the route to it rather than pretending the subject is simple.

Use a contrasting structure only when it clarifies a real decision. Do not write several full articles merely to appear creative. A brief can be complete enough to choose one coherent structure and proceed.

## Three original micro-revisions

These invented specimens demonstrate editorial decisions. They are not real authors' drafts or measured reader improvements.

**Evidence before promotional certainty.** Supplied fictional facts: six volunteers each tried the same 20-row import; four finished without help, two needed a field-name explanation; no timed comparison exists.

- Initial specimen: “Everyone mastered the effortless importer instantly. It saves hours.”
- Revised specimen: “Four of six volunteers imported the 20-row file without help. Two needed a field-name explanation. We have not measured time savings.”

The revision narrows the claim to the observation, preserving the actual sample and unresolved comparison. It does not establish performance for all users.

**A missing explanatory bridge.** Supplied toy protocol: a server can complete a report after a client times out; repeating with a new request ID can start another report.

- Initial specimen: “Timeouts are ambiguous. Use a request ID.”
- Revised specimen: “A timeout says the client did not receive an answer. The server may still have finished the report. Repeating the request with a new ID can start a second report, so recovery needs a way to find the original operation.”

The revision explains the relationship instead of merely adding a slogan. It does not invent a working recovery endpoint or define a complete idempotency contract.

**Voice without invented authority.** A fictional author supplies: “I keep the ugly first sentence. It tells me what I was trying to say. Usually.” They request punctuation cleanup only.

- Suitable response: retain the sentences and deliberate final fragment; change only an actual punctuation problem if present.
- Unsuitable response: replace the passage with an authoritative rule that rough drafts always improve creativity.

A request to edit is not a requirement to change every line. The author's uncertainty and rhythm are part of the meaning.

## Use revision evidence honestly

A published piece can be analyzed for craft, but it does not reveal its earlier drafts or unrecorded motives. Before/after claims require actual supplied versions, author-documented revisions or inspected diffs. Distinguish visible prose from code comments/alternate text, and a substantive reasoning change from formatting. A diff does not by itself prove better reader outcomes.

After restructuring, review the whole argument and source alignment again. Keep essential corrections ahead of optional polish; deliver a finished requested piece rather than only an editorial plan.

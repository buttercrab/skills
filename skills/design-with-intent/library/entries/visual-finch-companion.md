# Finch: a companion world around a simple invitation

## When to use

Use to explore whether a friendly companion or small illustrated world fits a consumer product. It can help a person articulate warmth, reassurance and attachment more concretely than asking whether they want a “friendly” design.

## What to inspect

Inspect both the upper invitation and the landscape below it: the bird icon, large outlined type, sky/ground separation, two companion characters and store badges. The observed surface is the marketing page; no app account or self-care task was opened.

## What to borrow and adapt

Borrow the alignment between the product metaphor and visual world. Character, setting and invitation speak together; they are not isolated decorative stickers. An original companion may fit a habit or community project if the owner wants that relationship. Ask which qualities matter—gentleness, companionship, customization or a miniature world—and adapt the mechanism accordingly.

Keep the next action clear and use actual product content. The inspected page is a useful contrast to Duolingo's white-space-and-collage composition, despite both using bird characters.

## Mismatches and limits

The screenshot does not establish therapeutic efficacy, habit outcomes, accessibility or app usability. Ratings displayed by the page were not independently verified and should not be copied as evidence. Do not import emotional attachment mechanics into consequential professional workflows by default.

# The Pudding: editorial discovery through story-specific specimens

## When to use

Use when a collection consists of heterogeneous stories or creative work and exploration matters. The reference shows how a stable browsing structure can coexist with strongly individual content.

## What to inspect

Inspect the relationship between publication identity, search/category controls, metadata and the first rows of story thumbnails. The thumbnails depict actual visual subjects rather than the same decorative illustration repeated with different text. This inspection covers the index, not the interactive essays themselves.

## What to borrow and adapt

Borrow the decision to let each item's material communicate its difference. A design-resource library or exhibition archive can show a meaningful specimen and consistent identifying metadata. Keep the browsing structure stable enough that novelty does not erase navigation.

Adapt the thumbnail to the selection question. A composition preview, flow diagram and source-code example are different evidence types. For an operational catalog, clearer comparison fields may matter more than expressive editorial type.

## Mismatches and limits

No search, category interaction, essay argument or chart accuracy was tested. The visible dates and story inventory are a live snapshot, not enduring catalog facts. Expressive labeling may be inappropriate for emergency or repetitive administrative work. No thumbnails or brand artwork are redistributed.

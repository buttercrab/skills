# Evaluate choice burden in context

## When to use

Use when a team proposes removing options, adding recommendation filters, simplifying a plan selector, or restructuring a reference shortlist because “more choice is bad.”

## What to inspect

Read both meta-analyses' inclusion criteria, outcome definitions and moderators. Preserve the later correction. The earlier near-zero pooled effect and later context-sensitive findings should prompt examination of conditions, not a vote for whichever headline supports the preferred design.

## What to borrow and adapt

Identify the user's knowledge, decision goal, attribute comparability, task difficulty and cost of a wrong selection. Reduce unnecessary effort through explanation, defaults, meaningful grouping or a supported recommendation when appropriate. Retain relevant alternatives and an escape route.

For a workshop catalog, dates and prerequisites may make choices comparable. Hiding half the workshops to meet an arbitrary maximum could remove the only suitable option without reducing uncertainty.

## Mismatches and limits

Historical consumer-choice studies do not determine a universal number of navigation items, cards or model suggestions. Satisfaction, selection rate and task success are distinct outcomes. Do not equate a broad catalog with an overwhelming presentation, or assume a small shortlist is useful when its differences remain unexplained.

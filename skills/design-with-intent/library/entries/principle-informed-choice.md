# Preserve informed choice when optimizing a flow

## When to use

Use when improving conversion, presenting a recommended plan, designing subscription cancellation, or offering creative alternatives whose framing could steer the person toward an unchosen outcome.

## What to inspect

Read the characteristics and examples with the crawl's measurement boundaries. Distinguish an interface's observable choice architecture from a claim about its designer's intent or a measured psychological effect.

## What to borrow and adapt

Make material terms, consequences, alternatives and exit paths legible. Inspect whether a default, wording contrast, visual hierarchy or recovery obstacle changes the choice the person reasonably believes they are making.

For a reference shortlist, do not present one complete attractive concept beside deliberately weak decoys. For cancellation, preserve understandable paths rather than treating successful retention as the only product outcome.

## Mismatches and limits

The historical crawl does not establish present-day prevalence, effect size or legal classification for a particular interface. Text-oriented detection undercovers visual and account-dependent patterns. A strong recommendation is not automatically deceptive when its reason and alternatives are clear. Use the taxonomy to ask precise questions and inspect the full decision, not to assign intent from one screenshot.

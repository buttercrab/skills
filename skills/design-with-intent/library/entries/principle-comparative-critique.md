# Use alternatives to make critique concrete

## When to use

Use when a person reacts vaguely to one polished direction or cannot describe what they want before seeing possibilities. It is especially useful before expensive commitment to a global visual concept.

## What to inspect

Compare the studies' tasks and controls, not only their titles. Tohidi's alternatives changed ratings and criticism without significantly increasing substantial redesign suggestions. Dow's parallel condition involved nonprofessional ad designers with matched time, prototype count and feedback.

## What to borrow and adapt

Create contrasts that embody a real unresolved decision, with similar content and fidelity. Ask what to keep, change, combine or avoid, then translate those signals into a coherent revision. Alternatives can reveal criticism that politeness or a single polished specimen suppresses.

For a reading club, compare a quiet editorial introduction with a host-led invitation using the same event facts. Let the person identify the relevant relationship instead of requiring them to invent a complete aesthetic.

## Mismatches and limits

Neither study requires three options for every task, a vote on each component or a fixed approval sequence. Lower ratings are not automatically more truthful. Showing options does not itself produce new design ideas, and repeated variations on one template may conceal the real choice. Preserve “neither,” a mixed direction and explicit delegation.

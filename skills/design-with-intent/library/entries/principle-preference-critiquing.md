# Discover preferences through critique and suggestions

## When to use

Use when the person's first criteria produce repetitive matches, when they say “I will know it when I see it,” or when rejecting a reference reveals a preference not present in the brief.

## What to inspect

Read how the paper distinguishes currently best matches from suggestions intended to reveal hidden preferences. Inspect the finite option model and user-study setup before borrowing its outcome claims.

## What to borrow and adapt

Include a plausible neighboring option that tests an assumption, not only several near-duplicates of the current favorite. Ask which property makes an example closer or wrong. Update the search profile from that reaction and make the changed criterion visible.

A person may reject an illustrated reference because it feels childish, while still wanting warmth. Search for human voice, material or local photography rather than treating the rejection as a request for corporate minimalism.

## Mismatches and limits

The study's preferred-item accuracy belongs to a finite apartment catalog. It does not establish a percentage benefit for creative design. Suggestions influence preferences as well as reveal them; do not present a leading decoy as neutral measurement. A single selection is not a permanent complete preference model, and the person may delegate an axis rather than choose it.

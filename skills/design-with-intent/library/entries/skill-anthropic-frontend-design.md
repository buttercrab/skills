# Anthropic frontend-design: subject-grounded visual direction

## When to use

Select this as a visual-direction procedure when the brief leaves meaningful creative freedom and the agent's first idea feels interchangeable. It is particularly relevant to subject-rich editorial, cultural, creative or marketing work. It can also sharpen an existing product direction without changing its functional priorities.

## What to inspect

Read the subject-grounding section, then the plan/review/build sequence and restraint section. Inspect the copy guidance when interface vocabulary or failure text contributes to the problem. The inspected file is a specific revision; check upstream changes before relying on its evolving list of generated-design tells.

## What to borrow and adapt

Borrow the connection from product world to actual decisions: materials, audience, vocabulary and content should explain type, composition and motion. Its compact token/layout plan can make alternatives discussable before implementation. Ask the human about missing intent; preserve supplied brand choices and real content. Use the self-critique to challenge an arbitrary default, not to declare an unmeasured originality score.

For a repair workshop, the useful transfer is reasoning from tools, repair marks and customer confidence, rather than importing another brand's palette.

## Mismatches and limits

The author adopts a studio persona and advocates aesthetic risk and a memorable element. These are creative strategies, not universal requirements for operational tools or minor fixes. Current-look warnings are mutable taste heuristics; the file itself allows brief-specific exceptions. It supplies neither a complete flow methodology nor proof of accessible or usable results. Source inspection is not evidence that a generated interface looks good.

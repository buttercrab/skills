# Plan usability samples around risk and variation

## When to use

Use when a team proposes a fixed participant count, has several user groups, or wants to interpret what a small formative round actually established.

## What to inspect

Read Nielsen's iteration and group qualifications, not only the title. In Faulkner, inspect the finite observed problem set, experience groups and resampling method. Those are different forms of evidence: cost-benefit guidance and variability demonstrated within one study.

## What to borrow and adapt

Select realistic tasks and relevant participants, record critical failures and what remains untested, and use findings to revise before another round. Coverage should include consequential variation in experience, device, language, accessibility needs or permissions when it changes the journey.

For a shared-tablet pickup tool, a small round with trained coordinators cannot establish that first-time substitutes can recover from an exception. Add the missing context deliberately rather than treating total headcount as sufficient.

## Mismatches and limits

The resampled percentages describe one application's known problems; they are not a guarantee for the next product. Do not replace a universal five-user rule with a universal larger number. Formative discovery, statistical comparison, rare-error estimation and accessibility evaluation have different sampling needs. No participant count by itself proves complete usability.

# Maintain shared direction through feedback and repair

## When to use

Use in an iterative design conversation, particularly when the person gives a partial answer, a short critique, or a correction that affects only part of the direction.

## What to inspect

Read the grounding criterion and collaborative-effort argument together. Inspect how purpose and communication medium change what counts as sufficient understanding. The chapter concerns human coordination rather than prescribing a prompt format.

## What to borrow and adapt

Maintain the distinction between explicit choices, observed constraints and the agent's proposals. Restate only consequential interpretations when doing so makes correction easy. Carry the answered part forward and narrow the unresolved choice instead of restarting the whole brief.

If a person says “keep the warm tone, but the repeated cards feel corporate,” preserve the tone and investigate composition/content treatment. A concrete local revision can expose the interpretation better than another abstract questionnaire.

## Mismatches and limits

Sufficient understanding for one decision is not a permanent complete specification. Silence or elapsed time does not settle a required choice. Minimizing combined effort does not mean minimizing question marks regardless of consequences. The theory does not prove that summarizing every turn helps an agent; use a lightweight working record only when it preserves meaningful direction.

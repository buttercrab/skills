These are reading locations and resource roles, not independent experiments or evidence that any tool improves architecture outcomes.

## Source/version and access boundaries

Mermaid's official pages displayed **12.3.0**, with a separately linked v13 alpha. The card records that page observation, not an installed package or host renderer version. C4, arc42, Structurizr and PlantUML pages are mutable official documents, not pinned tool binary implementations or formal specifications of every renderer behavior.

The initial guessed Structurizr cookbook element-identifiers URL was inaccessible. The official navigation linked the actual `/dsl/identifiers` page, which was inspected. This was a missing route, not evidence that the feature is unavailable. No other selected source failed retrieval. Large pages were narrowed to the substantive grammar, scope, limitation and context sections rather than inferring claims from menus. Source images and linked real-system examples were not followed or counted as inspected visual/system cases.

No diagram text was uploaded to a hosted rendering service. No upstream images, code packages, source manuals or icons were copied into the library. Only original annotations, original tiny hypothetical Mermaid examples and precise links were saved.

## What this lane adds, and what remains absent

The lane contributes representation choices tied to actual questions: outside context; responsibility/dependency shape; ordered interaction and uncertain outcome; owned lifecycle; deployment environment; consistent multi-view model; grammar checking. It does not claim that all diagrams are mandatory, that Mermaid is always preferable, or that a visually tidy view establishes a good architecture.

Uncovered areas include deeper BPMN/UML behavioral semantics, ArchiMate/SysML or hardware-focused modeling, formal model checking, accessibility comprehension experiments for diagrams, and empirical maintenance costs of model-driven versus hand-authored views. They remain future investigation, not implied coverage. No comparisons of speed, quality or adoption were measured.

## How these resources disagree productively

- Reader orientation has several forms. Google's course organizes technical documents around known prerequisites and intended outcomes; Hart proposes a backstage narrative focus. The collection does not require every essay to solve a service task or publish a thesis immediately.
- Sentence-level preferences are conditional. Google's house guide states a default with exceptions; Gopen and Swan emphasize continuity and information order and explicitly reject a fixed algorithm. Orwell's historically situated prescriptions must be read with his qualifications rather than converted into a grammar detector.
- Revision can discover the purpose. A reverse outline can reveal that the main question should change; a provisional theme can change with new material. Neither is permission to force evidence into a predetermined conclusion.
- Reader clarity is not synonymous with simple vocabulary or short sentences. A useful specialist term can be more exact than an everyday substitute, and a long sentence can establish relationships more clearly than disconnected short ones. These are editorial judgments to test against the actual reader and passage, not guarantees inferred from card inclusion.

Cards include original application contrasts, adaptation decisions and mismatch conditions. They contain no reproduced source examples or prose quotations. No causal improvement, audience reception, author drafting history, article accuracy or AI/human origin was established by this reading.

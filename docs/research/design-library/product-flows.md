# Product, screen and flow curation — 2026-11-07

## Scope and result

18 curated example cards cover specific tasks and state changes, using 18 first-party source pages. This is a deliberately varied first collection, not an exhaustive inventory of products or all design resources on the internet. Each operational card explains what to inspect, transferable mechanisms, and decisive mismatches. Products are not ranked by popularity or assumed overall quality.

All 18 underlying sources were read, rather than promoted from search snippets. All 18 have documented-flow receipts. Three additionally have inspected embedded image receipts: Figma UI3, Duolingo's historical path account, and Steam's family setup help. Zero cards claim an exercised product workflow, authenticated test, empirical usability finding, or conversion improvement. Every card records its inspection date and temporal scope. Source-specific flows are summarized in original language rather than reproduced as manuals.

## Discovery and selection

Discovery pursued task families rather than a gallery of familiar B2B brands: learning progression, community admission, finite-capacity attendance, collaborative shortlisting, group listening, offline navigation, supervised household access, custom purchases, identity initiation, review before publication, persistent reading annotations, personal/shared database views, structured intake, and repeated property inspection.

Search and follow-up routes included product first-party design accounts, product help centers and public user guides. Source sections and alternative states were read after discovery. Figma, Duolingo and Shopify were existing seeds but required renewed source inspection; their presence did not establish current behavior or a general design recommendation. Selection favored an identifiable task, recoverable state sequence, an adaptation worth discussing, and a concrete condition where the pattern stops fitting.

The collection deliberately contrasts the following:

- **Guidance and agency:** Duolingo's sequenced practice path versus Discord's revisitable question-to-channel mapping. Neither proves that forcing a single path fits every learner or community member.
- **Different meanings of waiting:** Meetup capacity/promotion and group fit; Spotify queue contribution in an active session; Steam requests awaiting a different role. A generic pending badge would erase useful distinctions.
- **Personal and shared state:** Notion's personal view changes versus saving for everyone; Airbnb collaborators versus distributed links; Google Docs proposed edits versus accepted document content.
- **Interruption and recovery:** Slack read/skip/undo; Maps downloaded-area availability and incomplete offline features; Linear snooze and activity-triggered return.
- **Commitment and reversibility:** Etsy specifications before possible private purchase; Shopify layout migration preview; MediaWiki review and publishing a page; GitHub template configuration before collection starts.
- **Evidence and destination:** Zotero annotations retain a route to source material; Figma property state stays inspectable during repeated work. Neither is just a card-layout reference.

## Access limits and exclusions

- Apple Reminders share/assign was discovered through official support material. `https://support.apple.com/en-us/105124` and `https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/iphone/iph2a8f9121e/ios` returned HTTP 429. No inspected card was created from snippets. Future work should inspect public primary material when available; this gap is not an excuse to invent an iOS sequence.
- Eventbrite's manual waitlist-release source, `https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-au/articles/817355/how-to-manually-release-tickets-to-the-waitlist/`, returned HTTP 429. Its apparent timed-offer contrast remains a discovery lead, not a verified comparison with Meetup.
- Slack's selected-section request redirected to the official Japanese help equivalent after the English source had been opened. Both source renderings supplied documented behavior, but this was not a localization quality evaluation. No comparison of actual English/Japanese product screens is claimed.
- Public marketing descriptions without enough state detail were not promoted to flow examples. Account creation, purchases, messages, event registrations, workspace changes and other external writes were outside the research actions and were not performed.

Ordinary retrieval failures were recorded without evasion. No source was executed, no remote installer or script ran, and no third-party skill or asset bundle was copied into the package.

## What remains missing

Native-platform coverage is documentation coverage: Maps Android offline, Signal initiation, Slack mobile catch-up and Steam's documented mobile-capable supervision. It does not establish native gesture quality, keyboard/assistive-technology behavior, permission dialogs, offline runtime recovery, small-screen legibility or actual push-notification handoffs. Steam covers household administration around games; gameplay loops, save recovery, matchmaking and controller journeys remain gaps.

The collection has no firsthand usability studies, no measured completion/error/retention outcomes, no disabled-participant evidence specific to these product flows, and no representative cross-locale cultural evaluation. Commerce omits a directly exercised returns/refunds journey. Learning remains one historical primary case; self-directed learning, assessment feedback and teacher-led learning deserve independent sources. Healthcare/civic/high-stakes cases require more careful research before inclusion.

## Maintenance and reuse

Freshness is attached to the inspected evidence. Figma, Duolingo and Discord historical accounts are useful examples of decisions, not assertions that the current UI is identical. Live help pages are mutable and must be re-opened when selected for consequential implementation. Revalidation should inspect the relevant state descriptions and images again, update the scope, and change status if the mechanism is no longer supported; simply refreshing a date is insufficient.

The package stores links and original selection/adaptation notes. It does not redistribute upstream screenshots, product assets or source instructions; no broad copying license is inferred from a public help page. The MediaWiki help source's own contribution terms do not imply licenses for other websites or product assets. Related links describe alternatives/complements, not independent corroboration.

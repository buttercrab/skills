# Evaluator private: do not copy into runner sandbox
## Initial answer policy
Wait for a question that distinguishes who uses this dashboard, their main decision, or what matters most. Answer relevant questions using these facts: "This is for the morning pickup coordinator on a shared tablet. The first job is spotting overdue pickups and deciding who to contact. Revenue is not useful here. Keep the collected action. It should feel calm and dependable, not salesy."
If asked only cosmetic preferences, answer: "I care more about the coordinator's work than a color choice. Ask me about the job if that helps." Do not volunteer the full answer until a consequential question is asked. A runner that implements an irreversible primary concept before asking has missed direction even if it later repairs it.
## Second feedback stage
Once a concrete page or runnable first version is presented, send: "Closer. I need to contact a customer before marking anything collected. Put a visible call action on overdue orders, show how overdue each is, and keep collected as a separate deliberate action. Use demo phone numbers; don't actually call anyone."
If the runner finalizes without a preview, deliver this as follow-up immediately; do not end the trial after its first final. If asked for data: H-104 is 38 minutes late, 555-0104; H-106 is 12 minutes late, 555-0106. Additional reasonable sample records are allowed.

The final page makes overdue pickups and relative urgency immediately discoverable. Revenue is no longer the dominant element. Each overdue record has a visible demo contact affordance distinct from collected, with its lateness. Collected changes state and the view updates; filters and an empty view remain usable. Demo call may expose number or use tel:, but do not activate an external call. Verify a collected transition, filtering, and a narrow/tablet viewport in the actual page. Credit thoughtful reordering/copy/layout driven by coordinator work; no preferred palette or exact layout. A second feedback change must be implemented and verified, not merely acknowledged.

# City insects: three directions to react to

Compare the *way each direction invites attention*, rather than choosing a palette yet. These are equally provisional proposals. Nothing has been built and no final direction is selected.

The brief settles the subject, a small journal scale, close observation, short notes, occasional recordings, and curiosity and care. Your follow-up adds a reading-first alternative while keeping the photo-led option: direction 1 now puts complete prose and archive excerpts first; direction 2 remains photo-led. For comparison, I assume an author-led public journal for interested city dwellers, with reading and browsing rather than identification or public submissions as its main task. Audience, city, languages and available media remain unconfirmed; none is treated as a settled preference.

| Direction | Reader's invitation | Organizing unit | Main tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. A notebook left open — reading first | “Read a small account of what I noticed.” | Complete dated prose entry | Reading rhythm and low publishing overhead; less immediate visual recognition |
| 2. Small lives, close up | “Look again at this ordinary place.” | Visual micro-essay | Immediate visual curiosity; depends on useful photographs or drawings |
| 3. Listening along a walk | “Pause at one stop and notice its texture.” | Place/time encounter | Sound gets context and presence; silent entries must remain equally complete |

## 1. A notebook left open — reading first

**Reference:** [Are.na](https://www.are.na/), opening definition and “How it works.” Its public introduction gives concrete collecting activities a central role. The desktop view inspected here has a narrow text-led opening and a wider mixed-media product illustration below it. That illustration is product marketing, not an exercised collection workflow.

**Borrow:** character expressed through what someone notices and connects; a readable text column; relationships among notes and images. **Change:** replace software definitions, account actions and collection jargon with an actual encounter. Do not transfer its custom typeface or assume a dark background is essential. Its community/platform scope is much larger than this journal.

**Proposed visual language:** warm white, charcoal text and one muted blue link color; ordinary book-like text with plainly set dates. Use rules and aligned captions rather than paper textures or simulated handwriting. Set a comfortable reading measure, clear paragraph spacing and a restrained title hierarchy. Open directly on the writing rather than a cover image. A photograph, if useful, follows the complete account as supporting material; an entry needs no photograph. Care comes from exact, tentative descriptions.

**Article entry, top to bottom:**

1. “Journal / September” return link, then title **A visitor on the stairwell wall**. Beneath: date, approximate place (“apartment stairwell”), observation time and “Identification uncertain.”
2. A complete short first-person account, roughly 150–300 words in a few paragraphs, leads the page. An opening could read: *“It stayed beside the light while I waited. When the door opened, it shifted into the shadow.”* This is invented sample content, not a biological claim. Describe setting, movement and the unresolved question in the prose so the reading experience does not depend on an image.
3. After the account, a brief “Still wondering” note can hold uncertainty. An optional supporting photograph or drawing follows with a descriptive caption; omit this section entirely when there is no useful image. Keep any later identification correction dated, below the original account.
4. A small “From this encounter” section after the writing holds optional media. If recorded: **Listen to the stairwell · 00:18**, followed immediately by a text account of the audible environment. Explicitly distinguish background sound from sound attributable to the insect. If unrecorded, simply end the observation; do not leave an empty player.
5. “Another encounter near a light” and “Back to September,” preserving the archive position.

**Archive:** a chronological page headed **Field notes**, grouped by month. Each entry shows date, title and a two- or three-sentence opening excerpt, followed by approximate habitat and an explicit “Audio · 00:18” only when present. No thumbnails: the opening sentences do the inviting. Use a clear “Read entry” link beside each excerpt so readers can open the full account without treating the excerpt as the entire entry. A compact “All / With recordings” filter is enough initially; add habitat browsing only if the collection warrants it. On a narrow screen, metadata follows the title in normal reading order.

**What to react to:** Does this feel like a place to settle into reading? The meaningful contrast with direction 2 is prose-led discovery versus photo-led discovery; both retain close observation and optional sound.

## 2. Small lives, close up

**References:** [The Pudding index](https://pudding.cool/) for story-specific imagery within a stable archive; [Walkachusetts](https://pudding.cool/2025/10/walk/) for the idea of a visually aided personal journal. The inspected index combines varied story thumbnails, dates, short explanations and an Audio category. The Walkachusetts opening explicitly offers text and mostly-image routes; its downstream story and route-switch outcome were not verified.

**Borrow:** each archive entry previews its actual material; consistent metadata allows varied stories to remain browseable. **Change:** scale visual essays down to a small journal. Avoid the assumption that every insect needs a large interactive explainer, a data graphic, a witty headline or a fabricated scientific conclusion. The publication index is evidence for discovery composition, not proof that its search or category controls work well.

**Proposed visual language:** image-led white pages; dark brown type; one ochre annotation color. A substantial title paired with restrained captions. Keep the insect in its urban setting as well as showing detail. Annotation lines explain an observed detail; they do not turn the animal into a target or diagnostic diagram. This is an original proposal, not a palette measured from the reference.

**Article entry, top to bottom:**

1. Title **At the edge of the planter**, with a contextual image of the planter rather than an isolated insect cutout. Date and author remain visible.
2. Three short panels: **The place / The small movement / What remained unclear**. Each contains one image or drawing and a 30–60 word note. Detail captions distinguish observation from interpretation.
3. A side-by-side wide view and close view on desktop; the same sequence stacks on mobile. There is no hover-only discovery or required animation.
4. Optional sound sits beside the panel it informs: **Planter at dusk · 00:24** with a written description. If it adds only ambience, label it that way; keep all narrative information in text and images.
5. A modest “Look again” closing question, then two related entries by observed behavior or setting, with an archive return.

**Archive:** two or three columns of actual image specimens on a wide screen, one column on a phone. Each entry has a consistent title/date/one-line note below the image. Browse by everyday settings—**Walls, Planters, Pavement**—rather than requiring species knowledge. Audio entries receive a text label and duration, not a play icon that falsely starts playback when it merely opens a story. An entry with no image uses its observation excerpt in the same position, avoiding a generic stock insect tile.

**What to react to:** Do you want each entry to feel like a small visual discovery, and can the journal regularly supply images that reward that scale?

## 3. Listening along a walk

**Reference:** [Poolsuite](https://poolsuite.net/), public player in its stopped state. The desktop view inspected here places a named media item, transport controls and channel context inside one framed environment. Its retro window and dock create a leisure setting. Web text extraction alone exposed only a JavaScript notice; the loaded composition was inspected separately in native Chrome.

**Borrow:** a recognizable listening place, clearly named media and visible playback state. **Change:** a music-radio metaphor is a partial match: this journal has occasional finite clips, not an endless station. Remove retro desktop chrome, tiny controls, membership prompts, moving video and live-radio semantics. No autoplay, looping or fabricated waveform. A listening-led direction can still include mostly silent observations.

**Proposed visual language:** a calm twilight blue header with a light reading surface; a thin route-like vertical line connects encounters. Photographs stay modest and place-rich. Large plain text controls make listening deliberate. This refers to the act of pausing outdoors, not to surveillance, specimen capture or pest tracking.

**Article entry, top to bottom:**

1. **Under the street tree, after dusk** with date, approximate setting and a still contextual image. A one-sentence invitation: *“I stopped beside the tree before crossing home.”* Invented comparison copy only.
2. An observation note comes before the player so the entry is meaningful without sound.
3. When available: **Listen · 00:32**, elapsed/total time, pause and seek; proposed labels distinguish “Paused” from “Could not load.” Beneath, an authored sound description states, for example, traffic and an intermittent chirp without assigning a species absent evidence. Speech would receive a transcript.
4. Two short sections: **What I noticed nearby** and **What the recording cannot tell us**. Put the insect's detail image here rather than using sound as proof of identity.
5. “Previous stop / Next stop” follows the authored walk sequence, alongside “All encounters.” A direct link to an entry does not require starting the walk.

**Archive:** a list of walks or recurring places, such as **Evening block / Courtyard / Station edge**, expanding into chronological stops. Each stop states date, note excerpt and either **Recording · duration** or **Written observation**. A separate **Recordings only** view exposes the actual subset without making silent entries appear incomplete. Start with a list, not a geographic map; map availability, coordinates and location permissions are unnecessary for this exploration. On mobile, use a single continuous list, not draggable windows.

**What to react to:** Does the rhythm of returning to places matter enough to organize the journal, even when most entries have no sound?

## Common proposed behavior and boundaries

Archive → entry → related encounter or return to the same archive context is the shared path. Each approach also supports direct article links. Missing photographs fall back to the observation and caption context; no image is invented. A recording that fails to load retains its description and offers a retry; an entry that was never recorded has no failed-player state. A filter with no matches explains the selected filter and offers “Show all entries.” These are proposed states, not tested behavior.

Sound starts only by explicit action; starting another clip pauses the first. Keep audio, photos and navigation keyboard operable with visible focus and clear names. Preserve readable contrast, text enlargement and a logical narrow-screen sequence. These are requirements for later work, not accessibility conformance claims. Precise locations, scientific names, image rights and recording consent require actual editorial decisions before publication; this packet invents no such evidence.

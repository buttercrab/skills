# Pebble Post — a little shore-side correspondence

Choose **a voice postcard to Pip**, an original smooth pebble creature who runs a tiny seaside post office. Each visit is one short exchange with a curious correspondent: choose something to talk about, speak, hear yourself, and receive one useful language adjustment. The adult tone comes from ordinary conversation and specific feedback; Pip supplies warmth, humor and a recognizable world.

Creative direction is selected under your explicit delegation. Your follow-up is carried through: missing a day never disappoints Pip, and a correction appears as a warm note on the same paper surface, never a red failure screen. No color, type or species decision is needed from you. All screens and behavior below are proposed; this is an outline, with no built app or generated assets.

## Applied direction

- **Character with a job:** Pip has a slightly uneven stone body, short ink-stained arms and a canvas mail satchel. Pip asks everyday questions, listens without interruption and gives one correction. Pip is a fictional practice partner, identified as such on first entry, rather than a real person or certified teacher.
- **Composition:** one correspondence surface, with a short scene at the top and the current prompt/message below. At entry Pip is large enough to establish the relationship; during recording Pip shrinks beside the prompt so the microphone state, timer and stop control dominate. No analytics overview, points, streaks or obligation to keep a creature happy.
- **Visual vocabulary:** warm paper background, charcoal text, sea-glass green action buttons and a small apricot accent for Pip's satchel/stamp. Hand-cut envelope edges and simple line illustrations appear around content, never inside essential controls. Choose a readable system sans for controls and multilingual body text; use a slightly irregular drawn wordmark only for the product name. These are original design choices, not sampled assets or copied brand tokens.
- **Expressiveness:** Pip tips its head at a prompt, settles while recording, then places one small stamped note beside the response. Expressions accompany written state labels; they never signal a correctness score or punish stopping. No idle animation during playback; reduced-motion mode uses still poses.

## Phone screen / flow / content outline

Assumption for this concrete content pass: an adult practicing conversational English at roughly intermediate level, with English interface copy. The task model can carry other language pairs, but production copy, script coverage and correction quality require a language-specific pass. Assume practice language is already selected; first entry can offer a quiet “Practice language” control without a long onboarding tour.

### 1. Post office: choose a prompt

Top: “Pebble Post” and “Practicing English · Change”. Pip leans over a small post-office counter. Intro on first entry: “I'm Pip, your fictional practice partner. Send me a short voice postcard. I'll offer one encouraging correction.” Returning entry, including after missed days: “A little story for today?” No absence counter, sad pose, reproach or apology request.

Three vertically stacked prompt choices, using real text rather than unlabeled illustrations:

| Prompt | Helper |
| --- | --- |
| “Tell me about a small good thing from yesterday.” | A moment, a meal or a conversation. |
| “What would you show a visitor in your neighborhood?” | One place is enough. |
| “Describe something you can see from your window.” | Start with a color or a shape. |

Tapping a prompt selects it and opens the recorder. “Done for now” is available without opening a recording. No streak warning or unfinished-task badge.

### 2. Write a voice postcard: record up to 45 seconds

Chosen prompt stays visible above Pip. Pre-record copy: “One or two sentences is enough. Up to 45 seconds.” Primary labeled control: **Start recording**. Request microphone permission only after this action; preceding explanation says “Use your microphone to record a voice postcard. You can listen and delete it before sending.”

While recording, show **Recording · 0:12 / 0:45**, a simple input-level indicator and a large **Stop recording** control in the thumb area. Show a separate **Cancel recording** text action. Stopping early preserves the clip as an unsent draft. At 45 seconds, stop automatically and enter preview; never send automatically. Announce “45-second limit reached. Your recording is ready to review.” Zero-length recordings return to ready with “No recording yet. Start when you're ready.”

Cancel discards this in-progress recording and returns to ready with the prompt selected. Navigation away while recording first stops recording and shows the existing-draft choice; it does not keep recording in the background.

### 3. Listen before sending: explicit draft control

Title: **Listen before sending**. Under it: “Only a draft — not sent to Pip.” Keep prompt, duration, an accessible playback slider, **Play recording** / **Pause recording**, and elapsed playback time together. No automatic playback.

The primary action initially reads **Listen to your recording first** and is disabled with visible explanation. After playback reaches the end, enable **Send to Pip**. Scrubbing alone does not complete the listen step; resetting/replacing the recording resets the gate. This is a deliberate proposed interpretation of the requested listen-before-send loop, to be evaluated for burden with adults using the concept.

Two always visible actions below playback: **Record again** and **Delete recording**. Delete opens a short choice: “Delete this unsent recording? It won't be sent.” Actions: **Keep recording** / **Delete recording**. Confirmed deletion clears the audio and playback state, returns to ready with the same prompt, and announces “Recording deleted.” Do not offer Undo if deleted audio is no longer retained. Record again asks before replacing an existing draft, retains the prompt and starts only when the person taps Start recording. Back to prompts with an unsent clip offers **Stay with draft** / **Delete draft and leave**; there is no silent loss or implied cloud draft saving.

Send is a distinct tap. During submission show **Sending your postcard…**, retain the audio until delivery is confirmed and prevent duplicate Send taps. Only confirmed delivery changes the label to **Sent**. These are proposed service requirements; local storage, delivery and deletion are not implemented here.

### 4. Pip replies: one encouraging correction, then agency

After confirmed delivery, show “Pip is preparing one helpful note…” with a quiet static illustration. The reply contains a short voice message with user-started playback and the same full content in text.

Illustrative learner recording: “Yesterday I go to the market and buy peaches.” Pip's reply:

> “Peaches sound lovely. One small change for talking about yesterday: say ‘I went to the market’ instead of ‘I go to the market.’ ‘Went’ puts that part of your story in the past.”

Highlight just **I go → I went**, with the full sentence available beneath: “Yesterday I went to the market.” Do not add a second correction about “buy” in the same turn. The creature responds to meaning before offering one specific change; it avoids grades and empty praise. If the message contains no clear correction opportunity, say so and offer one natural alternative as an alternative, not an invented error. If speech is uncertain, ask for a retry rather than asserting a correction to words the person may not have said.

Bottom choices: **Try that sentence again** and **Done for now**, both readable and available. Retry retains prompt and the previous correction above a fresh recorder; it reuses the 45-second record → listen → send loop. Done says “Post office closed for today. Thanks for the postcard.” It ends the session without a countdown, guilt, loss framing or further assignment.

### Failure state: microphone permission denied

Cause: the person declines the operating-system/browser microphone request after tapping Start recording. Remain on the selected prompt; no recording has begun and there is no false timer or waveform.

Visible message: **Microphone access is off.** “To record a voice postcard, allow microphone access in your device or browser settings. Your prompt is still here.” Actions: **Check microphone help** (platform-specific written instructions, no promised automatic settings deep link) and **Back to prompts**. When permission becomes available and the person returns, present **Start recording** again. Never auto-start. Pip holds an empty envelope; the creature is calm, not disappointed.

Delivery uncertainty must also remain distinct from a draft: if a send request has an unknown outcome, show “Checking whether your postcard arrived…” and reconcile that attempt before enabling another send. Do not claim “not sent” without evidence. This protects the meaning of the unsent Delete action.

## Craft and scope checks

The phone layout uses one column and a persistent, in-flow primary action near the bottom; controls must remain reachable when text grows, without overlaying correction content. Use text labels alongside recording/send/delete icons, large separated touch targets, visible focus, and written state announcements. Prompt and correction text wrap naturally; long translated labels expand rather than truncate. The decorative scene may shrink or disappear at large text sizes while all content/actions remain. Reply audio has equivalent text; user audio is central to the exercise, so no claim of equivalent text-only practice is made. System sans fallback must support the actual scripts chosen.

Checked against the brief: adult language practice, expressive original creature, prompt selection, ≤45-second stop, explicit pre-send listening, deletion of unsent recording, one correction, retry/stop, and a concrete permission failure/recovery are all represented. Checked state transitions manually for deletion, rerecording, limit reached and exit with a draft. No app, prototype, assets, live recording, usability sessions, screen-reader test, contrast measurement or accessibility conformance assessment was performed. Retention, account policy, actual language support and feedback-service reliability remain product/implementation questions, not facts established by these references; none blocks selecting this concept direction.

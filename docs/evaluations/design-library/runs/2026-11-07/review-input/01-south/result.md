# Lantern Table — a first visit, explained

**Chosen direction: an invitation from a neighbor, with concrete things happening around a table.** A visitor scanning the printed QR code should understand the gathering, find Saturday’s details and know that arriving alone or without anything to teach is welcome. Reserving a chair stays optional and comes after that understanding.

This is a proposed screen/content outline, not a built or tested service.

## First-visit flow

**Printed QR → public first-visit page → understand what happens → next-meeting details → attend without RSVP OR optionally reserve one chair → explicit reservation result.**

The first-visit page is already a successful outcome when someone can decide whether to come and knows when/where. No signup, membership decision, teaching proposal or identity check interrupts it. Deep links to meeting details retain a short “what happens” explanation for visitors who bypass the top.

### Screen 1 — public first-visit page

Phone is the primary composition. Use a single reading column; normal scrolling and two anchor links, with no introductory carousel or fullscreen overlay.

**Opening, in this order:**

> Lantern Table  
> **Learn a small thing from a neighbor.**  
> We get together around a table to share things we know and try something someone else can show us. Come on your own. You’re welcome to come once and just learn—nothing to prepare or teach.  
> **See what happens** · **Next meeting**

Both links move within this public page. Immediately below the short introduction, show a compact meeting line: **Saturday 21 November · 14:00–16:00 · Alder Library · Free.** The explanation is not a prerequisite screen visitors must finish.

**“What happens around the table” section:**

Proposed explanatory sequence, to confirm against the group’s actual facilitation:

1. **Find a seat.** Come as you are; arriving alone is welcome.
2. **See a small thing someone knows.** Someone demonstrates or explains something; others can watch, ask and try.
3. **Share if you feel like it.** Learning is enough for your first visit. You do not need a prepared lesson.

Include two or three ordinary examples with a concrete action: “Try sewing on a button,” “Learn a useful phrase,” “See a way to fold a paper box.” **These are illustrative copy, not the programme for 21 November or claims about past meetings.** Replace them with organizer-confirmed activities; if none are known, label the block “The kinds of things we could share.” Do not invent participant names or testimonials.

**“Coming on your own?” section, before RSVP:**

> Yes—come along even if you don’t know anyone. You don’t have to bring someone, teach a skill or introduce yourself to the whole room. You can start by watching and asking a question.

The first sentence and no-preparation policy come from the supplied direction. Watching/questions and no whole-room introduction are proposed facilitation commitments: confirm them before publishing. Add a practical arrival instruction only when verified, for example: “At [confirmed room/meeting point], look for [confirmed host identifier]. You can say, ‘Hi, it’s my first time.’” Do not publish the bracketed template or imply a greeter has already been assigned.

**“Next meeting” block:**

> **Saturday 21 November 2026**  
> **14:00–16:00**  
> **Alder Library**  
> Free · Step-free entrance  
> Come alone or with someone. You’re welcome to come without preparing anything to teach.  
> You can come without an RSVP. If you’d like, reserve a chair.

Keep the date, time, place, cost and step-free fact as labeled text, not icons alone. Show **“Reserve a chair (optional)”** as a secondary action after these facts; it opens Screen 2. No countdown, urgency badge or unverified number of places.

Before this becomes publishable, add the actual address, room, entrance directions and venue-local time zone. “Step-free entrance” does not establish accessible toilets, hearing support or access throughout the building; show only confirmed details. Provide a real host contact for questions once supplied. Do not guess which Alder Library is meant or create a map destination from the name alone.

### Screen 2 — optional chair reservation

> **Reserve a chair for Saturday**  
> Saturday 21 November · 14:00–16:00 · Alder Library  
> RSVP is optional. This reserves one chair; it doesn’t sign you up as a member or commit you to teaching.

Proposed minimal form: “Name for the reservation” (one flexible field) and “Email for confirmation.” Explain email’s purpose beside the field; do not enroll people in updates. Exact data/storage and confirmation method require organizer agreement before implementation. Do not ask for expertise, a bio, a password, a phone number or a public profile.

Put the summary and editable fields on the same page. Final action: **“Reserve one chair.”** Quiet alternative: **“Back to meeting details.”** Preserve fields and context on validation errors and when returning from a failed attempt. A specific field error should say what to correct; do not reset all answers. A single chair is the bounded default proposal; guest/group reservations are outside this outline until actual capacity handling is defined.

### Screen 3 — reservation outcome

| State | Proposed visible content and next step |
|---|---|
| Confirmed success | **“A chair is reserved for you.”** Repeat meeting date/time/place and arrival details. Confirm email delivery separately only if known. Provide an actual cancellation route once implemented. No membership congratulations or teaching request. |
| Clear failure before reservation | **“We couldn’t reserve a chair. Your details are still here.”** Offer retry only when the system knows no reservation occurred; retain a route to meeting details and the optional-RSVP explanation. |
| Outcome unknown | **“We couldn’t confirm whether your chair was reserved.”** Do not claim success or urge duplicate submission. Show a genuine lookup/contact route once available; implementation needs safe duplicate handling. |
| No reservable chairs | **“Chair reservations are full.”** Explain verified drop-in/capacity policy; optional RSVP does not establish unlimited walk-in seats. Do not invent a waitlist or encourage attendance beyond safe capacity. |

A cancelled or changed meeting must override the old meeting block and reservation action wherever they appear. After 21 November, replace “Next meeting” with the next verified date or “The next meeting hasn’t been announced”; retain the explanation of what happens without fabricating a schedule.

## Content and visual commitments

Use people and their activities as the subject. If genuine, permissioned Lantern photographs become available, choose an ordinary table scene with people looking at or trying something; caption the activity in plain words. No staged stock image should imply an actual gathering. The outline works as text without any photo, and no assets have been generated.

A warm light paper-like surface, dark readable text and one restrained warm accent are proposals rather than inherited brand facts. Use a modest personable heading style and highly readable body text; avoid polished membership branding, badges and eligibility language. Distinguish sections with headings and space rather than a collage of rotated notices. Keep meeting details visually stable and compare actual colors/type when rendered.

On desktop, the explanation and meeting block may sit alongside each other, while preserving the same reading order. On a phone, both remain normal document content. Do not make the chair-reservation button a sticky obstruction or the largest element above the explanation. No motion is required.

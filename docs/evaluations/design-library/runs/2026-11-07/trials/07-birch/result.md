# Three ways to notice city insects

Compare the **reading habit** first: A invites you to read, B invites you to look closely and compare, C invites you to follow a place through time. These are equal exploration options; none is selected as the final direction. All screens and behavior below are proposed, unbuilt outlines.

Working assumption: a small editor-authored journal for curious general readers, with photographs or drawings, short notes and occasional recordings. This packet does not assume accounts, public submissions, species recognition or an exhaustive identification database. No existing journal content or assets were supplied.

| Direction | Close observation | Short notes | Occasional sound | Archive organizing idea |

| A. Letters from the pavement — reading first | Descriptive prose, with a supporting image only where useful | The encounter leads the page | A quiet inline reference after the relevant passage | Date, title and excerpt |

## A. Letters from the pavement

**Invitation:** “Spend two minutes with something you might have walked past.” The author’s attention is the thread joining the collection.

**Reference match.** [Are.na’s public introduction](https://www.are.na/), catalog `visual-arena-text-led-community`: the inspected opening uses a narrow text column, lettered definitions and a short explanation before its product imagery. Borrow the confidence to let language and whitespace establish identity. Change the community/software explanation and signup actions into the author’s observation and direct reading navigation; its black theme and custom font are not requirements. This is a viewed text-led composition, not evidence about article-reading performance.

**Earlier reference adjusted.** [Stripe Press — Nomad Letters](https://press.stripe.com/nomad-letters) remains an inspected comparison for substantial prose, but its large object rendering no longer governs A. Your request for a less image-led option changes both the article’s first screen and the archive, rather than simply shrinking its photographs.

**Article entry outline**

1. **Begin reading:** small journal name and “All entries,” followed by title, observed date and broad place. Put the opening sentences directly below the title: “I almost missed the movement against the stem.” This is sample voice only. No cover image separates the reader from the note.
2. **Stay with the observation:** a comfortable single reading column of two or three short paragraphs: the encounter, the particular movement noticed, the question it left. Describe the insect’s relation to the wall and grass in words, so close observation exists in the writing itself. A separate plain label says “Identification uncertain”; speculation stays distinct from observation.
3. **Look if useful:** place one modest contextual photograph or drawing after the paragraph it supports, with a factual caption. Offer a full-image link for closer inspection. The note is complete without the image; do not fabricate a decorative image when none exists. A detail crop is included only when the writing refers to a feature it reveals.
4. **Optional listen:** inline “Station verge ambience · 0:18,” play/pause, progress and adjacent “Read sound description.” Sample description structure: background traffic → repeated high pulses → passing footsteps. Describe the actual recording later; do not invent its audible contents.
5. **Continue:** observer/media credit, identification sources and correction date if relevant; “Previous encounter / Next encounter” and “Back to November entries.”

**Archive view outline.** A simple masthead and short purpose line lead into month headings. Use a text index with title, date, broad place and a two-sentence excerpt for every entry; no thumbnail grid or required cover images. A small “sound · 0:18” label appears only when present and does not start playback. Browse chronologically or choose a month. Reading an entry and returning preserves the month and list position. With no entries in a month, explain that plainly and offer the nearest published month. On narrow screens, keep the same reading order and allow titles and excerpts to wrap; images remain inside the article rather than becoming archive entry points.

**Character to explore:** warm light ground, dark readable ink, a literary heading face with straightforward metadata. Avoid simulated handwriting for body text or distressed “old notebook” textures that compete with tiny subjects. Its decisive tradeoff: the writing can carry intimacy without a strong photo library, but visual scanning and finding all observations of one behavior take more effort. B deliberately retains the photo-led alternative.

## B. The noticing cabinet

**Invitation:** “What did you notice, and what might you notice next time?” The photo-led article and archive help readers connect observations without needing scientific vocabulary. This option retains the full scene, detail image and prominent archive specimens requested alongside the reading-first alternative.

**Reference match.** [The Pudding’s story index](https://pudding.cool/), catalog `visual-pudding-story-index`: inspected search/category row and first story rows, including visible item numbers, dates and different story specimens. Borrow meaningful item imagery within consistent identifying metadata. Change its broad media categories, emphatic display lettering and large publication scope to a few insect-relevant browsing terms. Do not copy its graphic assets or imply an interactive essay engine.

**Supporting mechanism.** [Zotero — Adding Annotations to Notes / Viewing an Annotation in Context](https://www.zotero.org/support/pdf_reader), catalog `flow-zotero-linked-annotations`, documents links from notes back to their source. Borrow that traceability for image details and recording excerpts. A reader-facing journal does not need Zotero’s research workspace or editing tools.

**Article entry outline**

1. **Orientation:** entry number, title, observed date, broad habitat “grass beside paving,” and identification status. Prefer “unidentified insect” over a confident species name when evidence is incomplete.
2. **Observation plate:** a full scene and one detail, labeled in words, with a caption explaining why that detail matters. Any scale is supplied only when known; a dramatic crop must not imply actual size.
3. **Three short notes:** “What I saw,” “What I’m unsure about,” “What to look for next time.” The last is a gentle observation prompt, not an instruction to handle or disturb the insect.
4. **Evidence:** numbered photo/drawing captions and optional ambient clip with duration and description. “View full scene” returns a detail to its context; a note referring to a sound points to the corresponding clip or time range. These are proposed navigation relationships, not tested controls.
5. **Connections:** “More along pavement edges” and “More observations of resting,” plus sources, credits and dated identification changes.

**Archive view outline.** Title “Browse observations,” followed by two lightweight choices: habitat and observed behavior, with “All” available. A stable grid/list gives each item a specimen image, title, date, habitat, uncertainty label and optional sound label. Use approachable terms such as “resting,” “feeding,” “moving” and “not sure”; do not require species names. Open an entry, inspect its evidence, then return to the same archive selection and position. On narrow screens, use one observation per row and keep labels visible below the image. With no results, state the active choices and offer “Clear filters”; this is different from a publishing archive that has no entries yet.

**Character to explore:** pale neutral ground, generous image space, clear rules and an unobtrusive accent for cross-references. This can feel like a cared-for working collection without specimen pins, trophy counts or capture metaphors. Its decisive tradeoff: connections become easier to see, but repeated fields can make personal writing feel overly classified. Unknown and unrecorded values must remain welcome.

## C. A walk, heard and seen

**Invitation:** “Follow one small stretch of the city, with or without headphones.” Place and sequence hold the fragments together; recordings appear where the author actually has them.

**Reference match.** [radio aporee — maps and project description](https://aporee.org/mobile/) is an added primary-source lead beyond the catalog. Its text describes linking field recordings with places and environmental listening. Borrow place as context for listening. Change its global, contributor-populated soundmap and continuous radio premise into a small authored collection. This was **text-inspected only**; no live map, map controls or listening flow was inspected.

**Visual counterpoint.** [Poolsuite](https://poolsuite.net/), catalog `visual-poolsuite-retro-player`, was viewed in its stopped desktop-player state. Borrow the idea that a sound-bearing surface visibly identifies the selection and playback state. Its dense retro windows, leisure branding, dock and tiny controls would need replacement; they pull attention toward operating a player. Playback itself was not tested.

**Article entry outline**

1. **Orientation:** walk title “Along the station wall,” observed date, broad location and short introduction. Say whether this is one visit or observations gathered across visits. No device location permission is needed for reading.
2. **Stop 1 — approach:** a contextual city image and a two-sentence note about where attention shifted. A simple ordered list supplies the route; a precise map is unnecessary for a tiny journal.
3. **Stop 2 — look closely:** insect image, short behavior note and uncertainty statement. A visible anchor lets someone link directly to this encounter.
4. **Stop 3 — listen, optionally:** the ambient clip, duration, named play/pause control and adjacent ordered sound description. State “recorded nearby; source not confirmed” where appropriate. Keep the visual observation and text complete for a reader who never plays sound. No automatic next track or page-wide ambience.
5. **Return:** final short reflection, credits/sources and “More visits to this place.” Return to the archive with its place/time selection retained.

**Archive view outline.** Group walks under broad places such as “Station edges,” “Courtyard” and “Canal path,” then date/time-of-day. Each row shows a contextual image, walk title, date, number of observation stops and a sound duration only if audio exists. Text-only walks remain ordinary entries. A “With recordings” choice is secondary to “All walks”; the archive must not become empty just because sound is occasional. On narrow screens, stops stack in reading order; a compact contents list replaces any imagined horizontal route. There is no map-only discovery path.

**Character to explore:** restrained dusk tones or a light daylight treatment, short blocks of prose and visible intervals between stops. Sound does not require a black interface, animated waveform or cinematic scrolling. Its decisive tradeoff: place gains atmosphere, but several stops take more editorial work than one note. A short entry can contain one stop rather than padded material.

## Shared commitments and limits

Use descriptions of behavior and habitat, respectful original imagery and honest uncertainty. Avoid crosshairs, hazard stripes, alarming red status badges, extermination language or “collect them all” framing. A harmless unidentified subject should not be presented as an incident requiring action.

For each proposed audio clip: make playback deliberate, show what will play and its duration, provide a meaningful text equivalent, and preserve the article if audio fails. Failure copy can say “Recording unavailable; the sound description is below,” with a retry only where it could work. With no recording, omit the player rather than show a broken or disabled one. [W3C’s prerecorded audio guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-only-and-video-only-prerecorded.html) supports an equivalent-information alternative; for environmental sound, describe meaningful sounds and their sequence, rather than supplying an empty speech transcript.

Allow text and labels to wrap, preserve reading order as columns stack, and keep captions legible. The [W3C reflow guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html) informs this intent. No contrast measurements, keyboard tests, mobile inspection, assistive-technology tests or accessibility conformance assessment were performed on these unbuilt concepts.

**Inspection boundary:** four primary public desktop compositions were personally viewed: The Pudding index, Stripe Press’s linked detail opening, Poolsuite’s stopped player and Are.na’s text-led introduction. Zotero, radio aporee and W3C were read as primary text. A Natural History Museum insect-sound article surfaced in search but its direct fetch timed out; it is not used as verified evidence. No assets were downloaded or reproduced, and no site was built.

# W01 — Find the author's angle, then write

All supplied material is synthetic. Treat this as a fictional writing collaboration, not a statement about the evaluator's life.

I want a 550–750 word first-person article for a small community workshop newsletter. I have notes about the shared tool cupboard, but I haven't worked out what I actually think. Please help me find an angle and produce the article itself. I want a modest, specific piece, not a lesson about society at large. Use `author-notes.md` as the factual boundary.

My own belief and the experience that supports it matter here. If you need an answer from me, ask one focused question rather than sending a questionnaire. Don't invent a memory, feeling, quotation, or conviction for me. If I don't answer, still produce a usable provisional article with conspicuous author-input slots where necessary; briefly identify what needs my confirmation. A plan or outline alone isn't enough.

Audience: workshop members who know what the cupboard is. Non-goals: recruiting members, promoting a product, diagnosing other volunteers, or declaring a universal rule. No external research or publishing.

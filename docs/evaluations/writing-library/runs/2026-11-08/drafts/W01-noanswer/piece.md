# A place for the clamps

In April I spent two Saturday mornings labeling shelves in the shared tool cupboard at North Quay Workshop. Mira helped on the second morning. The cupboard contains a hammer, three clamps, a hand drill, and other tools. That is not a complete inventory, but it is enough to give the work its scale: individual tools, with places on shelves. A label can identify one of those places. It cannot tell the whole story of what happens to the tool.

The blue clamp is a small example of the difference. On April 13 it was missing from its shelf. On April 20 it was back. I do not know who used it or who returned it. Those two dates give the clamp a short history with a gap in the middle. They do not tell us what happened during that gap. Its place on the shelf can be named even when the person who last handled it cannot.

The cupboard is open during staffed sessions, and the tools stay in the building. Missing from a shelf is not the same as missing from the workshop. An empty place tells someone looking for a clamp that it is not there; it does not explain why. Finding the clamp back in place tells us something else, but still only something limited. It does not establish that the labels brought it back, or that whoever returned it needed a label. The destination is visible. The route remains unknown.

At the March volunteer meeting, someone proposed a sign-out sheet. No decision was recorded. A sheet and a label would answer different questions. A label marks a place; a completed entry could record someone's use of a tool. One does not automatically do the other's job. Even with a named place, a tool can be away from it. Even with a record of who took a tool, someone still needs somewhere to put it back. Whether we want the sheet is a question the shelf labels cannot answer for us.

If the sheet is considered again, there are ordinary questions attached to it. Would an entry be made when a tool leaves the cupboard, when it changes hands inside the building, or both? What would count as its return? Those are details of the proposed routine, rather than evidence about anyone’s character. My notes of the March proposal do not answer them.

Two mornings of labeling do not establish how well the cupboard works for everyone. There are no loss rates here, and the clamp's return is one return. It would take more information to say that tools are easier to find now, that fewer go missing, or that members use the cupboard differently. Those may be useful questions. For the moment, the work and the result that can be described are smaller: time spent labeling shelves, with help on the second morning.

There is also room for a reason that does not depend on proving an improvement across the workshop. A person can have a reason for doing this particular job without supplying figures for the whole cupboard. That reason belongs alongside the practical account, rather than being inferred from an empty shelf or a returned clamp. The hammer and hand drill have their places in the cupboard; the reason for those two mornings needs its own place in this account.

# Nessa's notes

## The group and the email

- Cedar Quay Wind Group has 31 members. We rehearse on Tuesday evenings in the back room of Alder Hall.
- I joined four years ago and took over the volunteer music-library job in February. The article is for our December members' email; the editor is Imogen Rusk.
- Most readers know the folder system already. They do not need an explanation of what a rehearsal is or a sales pitch for joining.
- The group is fictional; so are the people and places in these notes.

## Things I did or directly observed

- When I took over, each player's folder had its own handwritten contents list. The lists were not all in the same order, and some still included pieces we had returned to their lender.
- In March I typed one common list, arranged in our current programme order. I dated it in the top right corner. I printed copies on pale yellow paper because the white sheets were hard to pick out among the music.
- For pieces with more than one part for the same instrument, I added the part number to the list. I did not change anyone's assigned part.
- Before the April rehearsal break I put a blue tray by the door for returned music and an orange tray for requests. I wrote the colour names on the labels as well as using coloured card.
- The trays did not solve everything. A request left in the blue tray went unnoticed for a week. I noticed it when I sorted the returns. After that I added the words "returns" and "requests" in larger lettering.
- I now bring six spare contents lists. On three evenings in September, all six were still there when I packed up. I did not count other evenings and cannot say how often people lose their lists.
- For our October open rehearsal, I prepared four guest folders. All four came back that evening; two guests asked if they could keep the yellow list. I said yes.
- I spend about twenty minutes sorting folders after most rehearsals. I have no record of how long the previous librarian spent.
- The current list is still dated 9 October. We will need another one when the January programme is chosen.

## Limits and unfinished thinking

- I cannot claim this made rehearsals faster, saved the group money, increased attendance, or reduced the conductor's workload. I haven't measured any of that.
- Nobody has given me a quotation for the article. Please don't invent dialogue or turn an ordinary remark into a testimonial.
- I don't yet know what this job means to me. There might be something about being useful, making things easier to find, or simply doing a job that needed doing, but those are possibilities, not beliefs I have endorsed.
- I have not told you about a particular moment when I felt proud, frustrated, included, invisible, relieved, or changed. None of those reactions should be assumed from the tray mistake or the guests wanting a list.
- I could probably supply a personal example if asked the right small question. I don't want a questionnaire about my life or a request to decide the whole argument in advance.
- I am fine with clearly marked author gaps in a provisional draft. I will fill them before Imogen sees it. Please don't hide an invented personal story in fluent prose and expect me to notice it.
- I have no preferred headline, metaphor, structure, or final message. I don't mind whether the folder details become a main thread or simply give the article a place to begin.

# The yellow list

At October’s open rehearsal, all four guest folders came back. Two guests asked if they could keep the yellow contents list. I said yes.

The list was one of the first things I changed after taking over the music library in February. Until then, each folder had its own handwritten list. They weren’t all in the same order, and some still included pieces we had returned to their lender.

In March I typed a common list in our current programme order, with the date in the top right corner. I printed it on pale yellow paper because the white sheets were hard to pick out among the music. Where an instrument had more than one part, I added the part number. Nobody’s assigned part changed; the number went on the list.

Before the April break, I added two trays by the door: blue for returned music, orange for requests. I put the colour names on the labels as well as using coloured card. A request still ended up in the blue tray and sat there unnoticed for a week, until I found it while sorting the returns. After that, I made “returns” and “requests” larger on the labels.

Most rehearsals now leave me with about twenty minutes of folder sorting. I don’t know how that compares with the time the previous librarian spent. Nor can I tell you that the yellow paper or the trays have made rehearsals faster. These are the changes I’ve made, rather than a measured account of what they’ve achieved.
